Irrigation in Australia

Australia irrigated 1.9 million hectares in 2020-21. Farms applied 7.8 million megalitres of water. That was 37% more than the year before. Where does it go, and where does it come from?

By Aida Ab Aziz (34597506)

1. The irrigation footprint

Pasture and cereals take up the most irrigated land. Cotton's area nearly quadrupled when rain returned.

About 80% of mapped irrigation is in the Murray-Darling Basin. Most of the rest is on the Queensland coast. Dashed lines show state borders. Next, see which crops grow there.

Sugar cane hugs the east coast. Cotton sits inland. Grapes and orchards cluster along the Murray.

Queensland has the most mapped crop area.

2. Which crops are thirstiest?

Cotton's water use rose from 0.4 to 1.3 million megalitres. Cotton now uses more water than fruit and nuts. Rice use rose almost nine-fold.

3. Where does the water come from?

Channels and pipelines are the biggest water source. River and channel supply jumped in wetter 2020-21.

Farms pump much of their own water. Groundwater was the bigger share every year.

4. Change over time

Water use fell during the recent drought. It rebounded as rain returned. The ABS changed its method in 2022-23.

Water per hectare has stayed fairly steady.

Rice varies most from year to year.
